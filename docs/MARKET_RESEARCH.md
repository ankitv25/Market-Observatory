# Market Observatory • Product research and design
November 7, 2026 • Proposed investment research workspace • Closing edition case study

## 1. The opening market board

A multi-asset manager should be able to scan six desks in thirty seconds, identify what changed, and open the desk that matters. The current dashboard gives its most valuable space to two US indices, crude, VIX and a sector grid. The proposed opening view dedicates a permanent row to each asset class. The rows keep their positions as markets move. Commentary follows the board. Sizing the interface this way makes it possible to see a quiet day in credit even when equities dominate the news.

**Desktop composition.** A session strip shows the last completed local session for Asia, Europe and the US. The left two-thirds contains six rows, each with two representative instruments, last level and units, change against the proper reference, one-month and six-month direction, a trend trace, and observation date. The right third contains three dated signals: participation and leadership, rates versus credit, and a change in cross-asset dependence. The next band contains the market reading and next releases. At 390 pixels, show six compact desk rows before commentary, prioritize level and move, and expose exact dates and additional horizons in the expanded row. Search is optional.

| Desk | Representative observations | What opens on selection |
|---|---|---|
| Equities | S&P 500 and a global or regional benchmark | Size × style, sector and industry leadership, concentration, earnings and valuation |
| Sovereign rates | US 2Y and 10Y yields and daily bp moves | Nominal and real curves, policy expectations, key-rate moves and auctions |
| Credit | IG and HY spreads when available, otherwise named ETF price proxies | Rating, maturity, spread, carry and stress by credit segment |
| FX | Dollar basket and USD/JPY | Currency direction, fixing, local vs USD return, carry and hedging |
| Commodities | Named WTI and copper contracts | Curve, inventory, positioning, volatility, volume and roll |
| Alternatives | Listed real estate and managed futures | Property types, infrastructure, gold, trend, private-market vintages |

**Edition test.** On November 6 the S&P 500 gained 0.31% while equal-weight RSP lost 0.38%. The Russell 2000 gained 0.89%, the Nikkei fell 5.96%, and the dollar index rose 0.74%. Intermediate Treasury ETF IEF lost 0.12%; the latest official US curve in that edition was dated November 5. Place those facts side by side, with units and dates. A broad “risk-on” label would hide both the geographic divergence and the difference between session clocks.

**Research design precedents.** TradingView exposes the variable controlling heatmap color and size; the desk board should likewise label what each visual encodes. J.P. Morgan organizes a substantial library by asset class and keeps persistent chart definitions across editions. Fidelity connects sector leadership to economic-cycle hypotheses. Our design adopts those decisions for navigation, repeatability and interpretation. ## 2. Equities, factors and valuation

**Start with participation across time.** The US index panel shows points and percent for the day. The global panel adds venue close, local currency and USD basis. Below it, display the full large/mid/small × value/blend/growth grid with one consistent index family. Each cell gives the benchmark, return type, 1D/1W/1M/3M/6M/YTD/1Y, drawdown and history through date. A size row reveals whether small companies participated; a style column reveals whether growth led across sizes. An unlicensed index can be represented by a named ETF proxy with the proxy's own units. The academic French size/book-to-market portfolios have a different construction and belong in factor research.

**A worked reading.** On November 6 large-cap growth IWF gained 1.26% and large-cap value IWD lost 0.36%. Growth led by 1.62 percentage points that day. Across six months, growth lost 8.73% while value gained 9.80%, a growth shortfall of 18.53 points. Small-cap IWM rose 0.99% that day but lost 4.58% across six months. A PM looking only at the daily sector winners could mistake a growth rebound for persistent leadership. Show a path of growth-minus-value spreads alongside the three size rows, then test whether earnings revisions or multiple expansion support a change in leadership.

**Industries, breadth and fundamentals.** Rank the 11 GICS sectors, then show their major industries. Maintain three distinct measures: equal-vote participation among point-in-time constituents, return of a traded sector proxy, and contribution to an index using dated weights. Present advance/decline, percent above 50/200-day averages, dispersion, sector weights and reconciliation residual. For earnings and multiples, expose trailing or forward basis, estimate vintage, cohort changes, negative earnings and missing coverage. Let the user compare a sector's move with revisions and valuation history. Morningstar's valuation-by-style presentation is a useful precedent. [Morningstar market outlook](https://www.morningstar.com/en-us/business/insights/research/stock-market-outlook)

**Factor theory inside the page.** Beta measures sensitivity to the selected market. Jensen's alpha is the intercept from a CAPM fit to excess total returns. Its interpretation depends on benchmark, currency, risk-free series, frequency and uncertainty. Add size, value, profitability, investment and optional momentum in a separately named joint regression. Display factor exposures, factor realizations, contributions, residual, sample size, confidence intervals, fit through date and R². Use realized attribution and predictive tests as separate panels. Reconcile realized excess return and hold an out-of-sample window for predictive claims. [French factor construction](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/Data_Library/f-f_5_factors_2x3.html) · [Academic size/value portfolios](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/Ken.French/Data_Library/six_portfolios.html) · [SEC filings API](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/sec-api-documentation)

## 3. Rates, credit and nine fixed-income analytics

The rates page starts with nominal and real curves at the current, previous, one-month and six-month dates. It shows key tenors, matched-tenor breakeven compensation, 2s10s and 5s30s slope, funding rates and issuance. The policy panel distinguishes FOMC decisions, individual projections, dated survey views and futures-implied paths. A Treasury par yield is a curve observation. Security-level valuation requires settlement conventions, dates, coupons, redemption, cash flows and an appropriate discount curve. Credit adds maturity-matched IG, HY, loans, agency MBS, municipal debt, securitized debt and EM hard/local-currency segments. Each series identifies the instrument or proxy and its valuation basis. [US Treasury methodology](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financing-the-government/interest-rate-statistics) · [Fed policy calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm)

| Analytics | What to show and how to verify it |
|---|---|
| 1 · Yield | Display YTM or YTW with settlement date, compounding convention and applicable exercise scenario. Reprice the stated cash flows. |
| 2 · Carry | Show accrual and coupon income over the horizon. State gross or explicitly funded basis. |
| 3 · Roll-down | Advance the security along the unchanged curve over the stated horizon. Separate carry before summing the two effects. |
| 4 · Spread / OAS | Name the reference curve, option assumptions and model. Compare like ratings, duration and currency. |
| 5 · Duration / DV01 | Show sensitivity in years and currency per bp for the specified position. Use effective measures where cash flows may change. |
| 6 · Key-rate DV01 | Bump tenors and show twist exposure. Compare summed partials with the parallel shock under the same bump convention. |
| 7 · Spread DV01 | Reprice spread changes while holding the rate curve fixed. Compare across credit buckets and stated positions. |
| 8 · Convexity | Compute central second-order sensitivity. State effective option/prepayment assumptions. Stress steepeners and parallel moves using full repricing. |
| 9 · Total-return attribution | Reconcile income, roll, rates, spread, FX and residual to realized total return on a stated reinvestment basis. |

**Numerical QA case.** A five-year option-free bond with $100 face, 5% semiannual coupons and 5% bond-equivalent yield, settled on a coupon date, prices at $100. Modified duration is 4.3760 years. A one-basis-point central price sensitivity has magnitude $0.04376 per $100 face. Full repricing at 6% yields $95.7349; at 4% it yields $104.4913. Run the example in the calculation gate. If only an ETF quote is available, publish its dated price move and leave unsupported bond metrics unavailable. [CFA Institute fixed-income risk measures](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/curve-based-and-empirical-fixed-income-risk-measures)

## 4. FX, commodities, alternatives and dependence

**Currency and global sessions.** On November 6 the dollar index rose 0.74% while USD/JPY declined 0.54%. The dollar basket and bilateral yen move describe different exposures. The FX page sets a consistent quote direction, identifies fixing times and separates spot return, forward carry and currency-hedged return. For a USD investor, multiply one plus local return by one plus the USD return of local currency, then subtract one. Align observation endpoints before attributing the interaction term. Trade-weighted indices add economic context. [BIS effective exchange rates](https://data.bis.org/topics/EER)

**Commodities and futures.** December WTI closed at $90.83 and January Brent at $98.32 in the edition. They have different delivery months. Compare WTI with its own prior close and show its calendar curve; a matched-month Brent–WTI comparison needs the same delivery period. Below price and curve, show inventory versus its seasonal range, refinery or end-use demand, dated positioning, open interest and volatility. A strategy chart must specify roll trades, multiplier, collateral and fees. Publish the timestamps of inventories and positioning. [EIA petroleum schedule](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/supply/weekly/schedule.php) · [CFTC release calendar](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/ReleaseSchedule/index.htm)

**Real estate and alternatives.** Give listed property its own section with performance by property type, FFO, same-store NOI, occupancy, debt structure and financing cost. Compare operating changes with price and Treasury sensitivity at their actual frequencies. Add listed infrastructure with revenue and financing sensitivities, managed futures with realized trend exposures, gold with real yields/dollar, and crypto with a declared UTC cutoff. Put private marks, flows, IRR/MOIC and valuation lag on a separate quarterly timeline. Quarterly underlying economics cannot be read from daily public proxies alone. [Nareit industry tracker](https://www.reit.com/data-research/reit-market-data/report/nareit-reit-industry-tracker)

**Worked dependence study.** Matched daily close-to-close price returns against the S&P 500, in adjacent 63-observation windows ending August 10 and November 6, give IEF +0.48 then +0.06; listed real estate VNQ −0.10 then +0.39; managed futures proxy DBMF +0.27 then +0.09; high yield HYG +0.69 then +0.55. The latest 21-observation S&P–IEF reading is −0.28. The magnitude and even sign depend on the horizon. Display rolling paths, sample and dates; positive stock–bond correlation may reflect common sensitivity to inflation or another shock. A causal reading needs additional evidence. Label price-return studies distinctly from total-return studies. [AQR stock–bond research](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/A-Changing-Stock-Bond-Correlation_JPM.pdf)

## 5. From observed market to house view

**One instrument, one traceable research chain.** Every desk scrolls from level and move to horizon, decomposition, economic mechanism, counterevidence, current view, and next release. Bring source results into the page with units and vintage. The source link belongs beside the measure. A PM should never have to leave the dashboard to find the explanation behind a headline. The morning edition emphasizes overnight sessions and upcoming releases. The closing edition reconciles prices and tests prior expectations. The weekly edition reviews unresolved theses against the editions that first recorded them.

**Worked intersection with the portfolios.** The November 6 SAA book assigned 35% of capital and 51% of modeled risk to US equities. Emerging equities carried 9% of capital and 16.6% of modeled risk. The relationship with bonds and listed property also changed between the two measured windows. A useful portfolio panel displays dated holdings, the covariance window, risk budget, and change in diversification alongside those market observations. Realized P&L attribution needs beginning-of-period exposures, return basis, FX and cash flows. Preserve a distinction between risk shares and contributions to actual return.

**Validation layers.** The data reviewer checks instrument identity, source permissions, calendar, timestamps and revisions. The quant reviewer independently recomputes arithmetic, factor fits, curve risk and correlations from fixtures. The PM reviewer performs a timed first-screen scan and two-click desk drilldown. The release reviewer checks the built artifact, the served edition and an actual browser rendering at phone, tablet and desktop widths. Each reviewer records evidence, severity, disposition and owner. No production sign-off is inferred from a design review.

**Release sequence.** First establish source rights, series registry, native units and freshness. Then release the six-desk opening board against real, historical cases. Next ship equity leadership and sovereign-rate depth; add credit, FX, futures and alternatives as their input gates pass. Last connect cross-asset risk, portfolio attribution and the thesis archive. Each phase is independently reviewable. Deliverable names, owners, dependencies, failure behavior and the shadow-run requirements appear in the execution plan.

**Evidence boundary.** November 6 is a historical worked example assembled from the dated, public project edition. The source observations are not a forecast. All nine bond analytics, complete size/style coverage, full sector constituents and institutional data entitlements remain project deliverables. Public display and archive permissions require confirmation per source and field.
